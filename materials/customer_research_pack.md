# Customer Research Pack

## Purpose

This document is designed to help the team satisfy the customer research, interview, and end-user profile requirements without inventing evidence. Use it to run real interviews, capture answers consistently, and translate findings into the final report and pitch.

## Interview Goals

The interviews should test the following hypotheses:

1. Workers in dynamic industrial environments frequently miss hazards that are outside their immediate field of view.
2. Safety managers believe situational awareness failures contribute to near misses, incidents, or costly work interruptions.
3. Existing controls such as training, PPE, spotters, and radios reduce risk but still leave meaningful gaps.
4. Buyers would consider funding a pilot if the device is easy to deploy and the value case is measurable.
5. Workers will only trust the product if alerts are timely, accurate, and not distracting.

## Consent Script

"We're working on a university entrepreneurship project about improving worker safety in industrial environments. We'd like to ask a few questions about how hazards are recognized on site and how companies currently manage those risks. Your responses may be summarized in our course project, but we will not quote sensitive information without permission. Are you comfortable continuing?"

## Interview Groups

Interview at least one person from each of these groups if possible:

- frontline worker or technician
- site supervisor, superintendent, or foreman
- safety coordinator, EHS manager, or operations leader

## Open-Ended Questions for Frontline Workers

1. Tell me about the types of hazards you have to watch for during a normal shift.
2. What is the hardest part about staying aware of your surroundings while also doing your job?
3. Can you walk me through a recent near miss, close call, or moment when something almost went wrong?
4. Which hazards are easiest to miss, and why do they get missed?
5. What tools, procedures, or people help you stay safe today?
6. Where do those current safety measures fall short?
7. How do new workers usually learn the high-risk areas or patterns on a site?
8. What would make you trust or distrust a wearable safety device that gives alerts?
9. What would make a device too annoying, distracting, or impractical to wear?
10. If a device could warn you about one kind of hazard reliably, which hazard would matter most?

## Open-Ended Questions for Safety Managers and Supervisors

1. Tell me about the incident patterns or near misses you worry about most on your site.
2. Where do situational awareness failures show up most often?
3. How do you currently reduce risks from moving equipment, blind spots, or restricted areas?
4. What are the limitations of spotters, supervision, training, or passive PPE in those situations?
5. How do you currently measure near misses or leading safety indicators?
6. What kinds of incidents create the biggest operational or financial impact?
7. How do new or temporary workers affect your safety risk profile?
8. What would you need to believe before approving a pilot of a wearable safety technology?
9. Who would need to sign off on a purchase or pilot?
10. If a pilot worked, what outcome would make you call it a success?

## Open-Ended Questions for Economic Buyers

1. Walk me through how a new safety technology is usually evaluated and approved in your organization.
2. What budget does a pilot like this usually come from?
3. Which outcomes matter most when you assess whether a safety tool is worth paying for?
4. What concerns would you have about privacy, labour relations, or implementation complexity?
5. Under what conditions would you expand from a pilot to a broader deployment?

## Draft Personas

### Persona A: New Contractor Worker

- Context: new to the site, task-focused, often around moving equipment
- Primary job: complete assigned work safely and stay productive
- Pain points: unfamiliar site patterns, blind spots, divided attention
- Success metric: fewer surprises and clearer awareness in the moment
- What they need from the product: comfort, low distraction, high alert accuracy

### Persona B: Safety Manager

- Context: accountable for workforce safety, compliance, and incident reduction
- Primary job: reduce exposure while keeping operations moving
- Pain points: limited visibility, inconsistent hazard recognition, expensive incidents
- Success metric: fewer near misses and better safety data
- What they need from the product: measurable value, easy deployment, credible reporting

## Evidence You Still Need Before Final Submission

- at least a small set of real interviews with permission
- one clear statement of who the economic buyer is
- one validated use case that buyers rank as most urgent
- evidence about acceptable price range or pilot budget
- evidence about privacy and adoption barriers
